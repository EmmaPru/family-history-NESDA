# Abstract

## Background

Genetic liability of depressive disorder can be captured by psychopathology in relatives (family history). Various methods summarize family history in a single score, differing in included information as well as underlying model.  We systematically compared the performance of family history indicators, including promising new indicators based on the liability threshold model, in predicting depressive disorder. 

## Methods 

We calculated selected family history indicators for depression (dichotomous, proportion, novel genetically-informed method PAFGRS) in 1339 participants of the Netherlands Study of Depression and Anxiety (Ncase= 1086). Polygenic scores were computed from the most recent GWAS for major depression. We assessed correlations between genetic liability indicators, as well as their prediction of lifetime depressive disorder diagnosis.

## Results 

Correlations of family history indicators with each other were high (r = 0.71 - 0.99), and much lower with the PGS (r = 0.15). There was a suggested increase in predictive accuracy for more elaborately computed scores, ranging from proportion (AUC = 0.66, OR = 2.26, 95%CI = 1.88-2.71) to PAFGRS (AUC = 0.70, OR =17.06, 95%CI = 9.46 - 30.77).  The best-performing family history indicator and the PGS were independently associated with depressive disorder (PAFGRS: OR = 15.17, 95%CI = 8.36-27.51, p = 3.59x10-19; PGS: OR = 1.30, 95%CI = 1.12-1.50, p = 0.0004).

## Conclusions 

Our analysis shows that more elaborate family history indicators, including family size, prevalence, heritability and based on genetic theory, would be preferrable over simpler methods. Family history and PGS were complementary in prediction, showing the added value of including both in future studies. 

# Scripts

## Core 

**setup.R**  
    1) installs and loads required packages  
    2) defines fixed parameters  
    3) defines additional functions used in simulations and analyses  
    Input: disorder_thresholds.RData (pre-calculated thresholds for ages 1-140, optionally generated by this script)  
    Output: parameters and functions 
    Status: Functional. Simulation functions should not be here, but in indicator_functions.R.

**indicator_functions.R**  
    1) sources setup.R to load packages and parameters  
    2) loads additionally required packages  
    3) defines functions to calculate individual family history indicators: FH+/-, Proportion, PAFGRS, FLS, score_FH (wrapper function)
    Input: setup.R  
    Output: functions  
    Status: Functional. Tested by test_indicator_functions.R  

**test-indicator_functions.R**  
    contains tests for functions defined in indicator_functions.R, namely:  
    FH_plusminus, case_proportion, apply_pafgrs, FLS, score_FH  


## NESDA Analysis

**assess_prediction.R**  
    1) full assessment of the performance of the indicators in predicting depression  
    2) modified list of assessment results for Figure 4, including combined models  
    Input: Scripts/setup.R, indicators_PRS_index.RData  
    Output: assessment.RData, assessment_display4.RData, assessment_display4_full_results.csv  
    Status: Running

**calculate_indicators.R**     
    1) prepares the dataframes for family history indicator calculation by separating for assessment type  
    2) calculates the family history indicators for depression only, and depression + anxiety  
    3) prepares a file with pident, PRS and FH indicators, excluding related individuals  
    Input: Scripts/indicator_functions.R, joint_fam_work_R2.csv, DAP2406_PRS_pident.sav, NESDA_lifetime_depression_cleaned.csv, FTI_famsize.csv, FTI_age.csv, FTI_diagnoses.csv  
    Output: Int_results/FH.RData, Int_results/indicators_PRS_index.RData, Int_results/indicators_with_index.RData  
    Status: Running  
    !! Contains pidents of related individuals !!

**NESDA_FTI_preprocessing_diagnoses.R**  
    1) assesses and processes FTI diagnoses for depression and anxiety  
    2) creates diagnoses based on simple and elaborate criteria, with and without imputation  
    3) compares prevalences and cross-tables of different assessment types
    Input: age.RData (from NESDA_FTI_preprocessing_general.R)  
    Output: FTI_diagnoses.csv (with diagnoses for depression and anxiety for each family member)  
    Status: A delicate construction.

**NESDA_FTI_preprocessing_general.R**  
    1) Reads in NESDA FTI data  
    2) Cleans and reshapes the data into a tidy format  
    3) Extracts family size information  
    4) Calculates ages of family members with specific conditions  
   Input: NESDA FTI raw data files, demographics, and interview info  
   Output: family size df, age df (with all FTI questions for further use)  
   Status: Messy but don't want to mess with it  

**NESDA_lifetime_diagnoses_W6.R**  
    generates lifetime diagnoses for NESDA participants by updating baseline up to Wave 6  
    for depression, anxiety and bipolar disorder  
    Input: diagnosis files from all waves  
    Output: NESDA_lifetime_diagnoses.csv (all controls) and NESDA_lifetime_depression_cleaned.csv (controls cleaned from anxiety/bipolar)  
    Status: Sure hope it runs.  

**visualize_results.Rmd**  
    creates the folllowing visualizations:  
    1) Descriptives of FTI data availability, also by assessment type  
    2) Prevalences of disorders in relatives  
    3) Descriptives of final sample (Table 1)  
    4) Plot of sex- and age-specific prevalences for PAFGRS+Sex/Age  
    5) Generation of full correlation matrices and heatmaps to display (Figure 3)  
    6) Distributions of indicators (Figure 2)  
    7) AUC plots (Figure 4)  
    8) OR plots (Figure 5)  
    Input: Scripts/indicator_functions.R, assessment.RData, indicators_PRS_index.RData, AUC_results_combined.RData, assessment_display4.RData, NESDA_DAP2406/N1_A100R.sav, FTI_famsize.csv, FH.RData  
    Output: Main and supplementary figures and tables (see final code chunk)  
    Status: Running
